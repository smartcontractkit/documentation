title: "Trust and failure handling"
description: "What a prediction market relies on when it uses the Prediction Market Resolution Engine, and what the engine does when evidence is missing, late, or contradictory."
---

The engine delays resolution rather than guessing an outcome or lowering its evidence requirements.

## What you rely on

| Dependency              | What it does                                   | How it is protected                                                                                                                           |
| ----------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Data providers          | Report the real-world result                   | Each market's measurement profile sets a quorum of agreeing providers; see below. Chainlink monitors providers for outages and discrepancies. |
| The DON                 | Fetches evidence and applies the market's rule | Every node fetches independently, and nodes must agree on what each provider returned. A hash of each outcome is recorded onchain.            |
| Chainlink control plane | Schedules checks and maintains the catalog     | It sends identifiers and rules only. It cannot supply evidence or choose an outcome. The DON keeps no state between runs.                     |
| The market's rule       | Turns agreed facts into an outcome             | Checked against historical events before coverage goes live.                                                                                  |

### Quorum

A market's measurement profile sets its quorum: the number of independent providers required to reach a decision. Most sports markets require two independent providers. A single authoritative source can be approved with quorum `1`.

Every provider whose value is counted must report it as final. If fewer than quorum usable providers remain, no outcome is delivered.

## Provider agreement and DON consensus

The engine applies two separate checks:

- **DON consensus** confirms what each provider said. Every node in the DON calls the provider and the nodes must report identical data, so no single node can misreport a provider's response. See [Consensus computing in CRE](/cre/concepts/consensus-computing).
- **Provider agreement** confirms that independent providers say the same thing. The workflow normalizes each provider's response into common facts, such as final runs per team, and accepts a fact only when enough providers report the same value.

DON consensus does not replace provider agreement. Many nodes reading one provider still produce one source. Provider agreement is what protects against a provider reporting a wrong result.

Agreement is checked on each fact the market needs. A missing value stays missing; it is never treated as zero. A value reported by fewer providers than the market's quorum cannot resolve it, however confident those providers are.

Agreement reduces source risk but does not remove it. Two providers can share an upstream feed or make the same error.

## When the engine withholds an outcome

The engine withholds an outcome when the evidence cannot support it. It never substitutes `NO` or another value. Its response depends on the failure:

| Situation                                                           | What the engine does                                                                                               | Event status                        |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------- |
| The event has not finished, or a provider's result is not yet final | Waits and checks again.                                                                                            | `scheduled`, `live`, or `postponed` |
| A provider is unavailable or returns an error                       | Retries automatically. The provider is excluded while it fails.                                                    | Unchanged                           |
| Providers report different final results                            | Fails closed for that market: delivers nothing, keeps checking for evidence, and escalates to Chainlink operators. | `final`; the market is unresolved   |
| The rule cannot decide, for example a tie the rule does not allow   | Fails closed for that market and escalates.                                                                        | `final`; the market is unresolved   |
| The event is cancelled                                              | Delivers no outcome.                                                                                               | `cancelled`                         |
| The event is postponed                                              | Moves the checks for every market on the event.                                                                    | `postponed`                         |

An ordinary fixture remains `final` while any unresolved markets await sufficient evidence. An unresolved market receives no outcome. Use `completed` as the reliable signal that resolution is done. See [Market lifecycle](/solutions/prediction-market-resolution-engine/reference/lifecycle#event-statuses) for event statuses.

## Timing

Resolution follows the real end of the event, not its scheduled start: the engine resolves once the data providers confirm a final result and agree. Overtime, extra innings, and delays move that point.

An outcome can take longer when providers are slow to mark a result final, when they disagree, or when a required statistic arrives late. After the engine delivers, your platform's own dispute window, if any, adds time before positions are final.

## Monitoring and disputes

Chainlink monitors resolution and escalates failures, including every unresolved market and event, to its operators.

Your platform runs its own dispute process for traders.
