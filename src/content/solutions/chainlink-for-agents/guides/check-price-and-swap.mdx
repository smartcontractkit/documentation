<Aside type="note" title="Prerequisites">
  This guide assumes that you have already linked your agent to the Chainlink platform using the steps in the [Link your agent to Chainlink guide](/solutions/chainlink-for-agents/guides/link-your-agent). You also need:

- A few USDC on Base left in your agent's address, to pay this guide's fees.
- About 6 USDC on Base in your own wallet. Later in this guide, you send it to your agent's chain wallet.

Your agent doesn't need any ETH: it pays its fees in USDC, and Chainlink covers the gas for its chain wallet.

</Aside>

In this tutorial, you will:

1. Set guardrails for your agent in the Chainlink platform UI.
2. Have your agent fetch live token prices from Chainlink's Data Streams.
3. Have your agent create its chain wallet on Base, and fund it with USDC.
4. Swap USDC for ETH on Base through Uniswap.
5. Check your agent's balances after the swap.
6. Watch a guardrail block a swap.

<Aside type="tip" title="Agent address vs. chain wallet">
  Your agent works with two addresses:

- **Agent address:** the address your agent created during sign-up, and the one you linked to your organization. It's your agent's identity, and your agent pays its Chainlink for Agents fees from it.
- **Chain wallet:** a [smart contract wallet](/crec/concepts/smart-accounts) that Chainlink deploys for your agent on each chain it uses, such as Base. It holds the tokens your agent swaps or sends. It has no private key of its own: it only carries out instructions signed with your agent's key and delivered by Chainlink.
  - We might also refer to it as an SVA (Signature Verifying Account).

Your agent's swaps run from its chain wallet, so Chainlink for Agents can check each one against your guardrails before it goes onchain. Both addresses belong to the same agent, so your link and your guardrails cover both.

</Aside>

## Set up guardrails

1. Go to the [Chainlink platform's agent dashboard](https://app.chain.link/cre/agents), and click on `Add Guardrails`.
2. Set up minimal guardrails that allow you to follow the tutorial below, without exposing a wider than needed permission set.

<ClickToZoom
  src="/images/solutions/chainlink-for-agents/check-price-and-swap/check-price-and-swap-1.png"
  alt="Screenshot of the Chainlink platform's guardrails panel for the agent. Agent Restriction is on. Send Limit is on, with a per-transaction limit of 10 USD and a 24-hour limit of 15 USD. />

3. Click on `Apply Settings` and make sure your guardrails are set up.

<Aside type="caution" title="Keep Agent Restriction on">
  We highly recommend leaving Agent Restriction on, even if you don't configure specific settings. It limits your agent
  to the workflows in the Chainlink catalog, even if your agent switches itself to unrestricted mode.
</Aside>

## Check the ETH price

This serves as the first test of your agent's connection to the Chainlink platform.

Give this prompt to your agent:

```
What's the current price of ETH in US dollars? Get it from Chainlink Data Streams through Chainlink for Agents.
```

The agent should be able to fetch the latest price straight from the platform via Chainlink Data Streams.
Simply reading the data doesn't require us to set up a separate chain wallet, but the next step does.

## Create and fund your agent's chain wallet

Give this prompt to your agent to create a fresh, separate chain wallet that it will use to carry out swaps and other onchain operations, bound by the guardrails set up by you in the UI:

```
Create a chain wallet for yourself on Base and give me its address.
```

Behind the scenes, Chainlink deploys the wallet contract on Base, and the agent pays a one-time fee for that deployment. It's the biggest fee-paying operation in this guide.

> Please note that since this is a contract deployment, the agent doesn't actually hold the private key to this account, and thus the address generation is non-deterministic.
> If your agent creates a new chain wallet on another chain, it will have a different address.

<ClickToZoom
  src="/images/solutions/chainlink-for-agents/check-price-and-swap/check-price-and-swap-3.png"
  alt="Screenshot showing the agent creating a new chain wallet on Base."
/>

- Send `6 USDC` to the freshly created chain wallet on `Base Mainnet`. You can use [BaseScan](https://basescan.org/) to track balances.

## Swap USDC for ETH using Uniswap

Remember the guardrails we set up earlier? We allowed our wallet to be able to swap tokens on Uniswap. Let us see if that works.

Give this prompt to your agent to initiate the swap:

```
Swap 5 USDC for ETH on Base from your chain wallet.

Before you sign, show me what I'll get and wait for my confirmation.
```

> You can also edit the prompt to have your agent simply execute the swap without asking for confirmation.

<ClickToZoom
  src="/images/solutions/chainlink-for-agents/check-price-and-swap/check-price-and-swap-4.png"
  alt="Screenshot showing the agent preparing to swap USDC for ETH on Uniswap."
/>

- Before moving forward, ask your agent to check if the swap went through, and make sure your balances reflect the updated amounts.

## Watch a guardrail block a swap

1. Go to the [Chainlink platform's agent dashboard](https://app.chain.link/cre/agents), and locate your agent.
2. Click on the `Settings` icon and configure your existing settings to turn off `Uniswap` token swaps. Click on `Apply Settings`.
3. Paste this prompt to try and swap your remaining `USDC` for `ETH` **after turning** off Uniswap swaps from inside the UI.

```
Swap 1 USDC for ETH on Base please.
```

Your agent should now refuse to execute the swap, as the guardrail for Uniswap swaps has been turned off.

## What's next

You've seen your guardrails allow one swap and block the next. From here, try changing one setting at a time and asking your agent for something new. Keep the amounts small while you experiment.

Some ideas:

- **Earn yield on Aave**: Turn on the `Aave - deposits and withdrawals` setting, then ask your agent to interact with the protocol.
- **Bridge using CCIP**: Turn on the setting and try sending a cross-chain swap. Maybe configure an allowlist and try sending tokens to a wallet that is **NOT** on the allowlist?

To see everything else your agent can do, go back to [What can your agent do](/solutions/chainlink-for-agents#what-can-your-agent-do).
